# SP-WP1.2: Opening office cutscene, complete first-pass script

**Status:** **Approved by project owner 12 October 2026** as GAME-SP1 opening baseline. Dialogue may be tightened during human playtesting without silently changing the approved story structure.  
**Screen ID:** OFFICE-INTRO  
**Target runtime:** 2 minutes 45 seconds to 3 minutes 30 seconds, skippable.  
**Input:** Click/tap to advance dialogue; optional skip / replay from menu.  
**Location:** An entirely fictional, unnamed modern office, with no recognisable employer branding.  
**Characters:** Stephen Martin (20s, energetic), David (30s, spectacularly self-confident), Paul (40s, clean-shaven, glasses, white polo, frequently expressionless).  
**Visual anchors:** Approved canonical Stephen, David and Paul sheets in \`public/assets/generated/characters/\`.

## Dramatic purpose

Establish the relationships in one efficient location, convey Stephen is leaving for a holiday in China, give David and Paul their distinctive comedy voices, set up the nonsensical bear-message motif, establish their song-writing hobby, and lay the groundwork for the "rescue" misunderstanding. Do not use a real employer name, genuine work building, private messages, actual relationship timeline, identifiable family information, or a real travel date.

The holiday and any boyfriend/family misunderstandings in subsequent scenes are openly **fictional**, and do not state facts about any real third party.

## Reusable stage elements (small asset budget)

- **Background OFFICE-BG-01:** A wholly invented generic shared office with three desks, a temperamental printer, exit door, wall clock and luggage beside Stephen's chair. No branded screens, signage, maps or identifiable architecture.
- **Characters:** Three approved character images, used as cropped torso sprites with a neutral pose and changing expression cut-outs. Can start with still portraits; animation is optional.
- **Props:** Laptop, printer paper, suitcase, fictional chat overlay, illustrated (non-copyright-copy) honey-loving cartoon bear sticker, a comically bad "threat assessment" sheet.
- **Audio:** Printer whirr, short chat ping, suitcase zip, two-second knowingly terrible goodbye song sting, comic sting into title card. All optional initially except readable dialogue.
- **Camera:** One static wide establishing frame and two portrait crops; simulate close-ups with framing and character emphasis, not unique illustrated backgrounds.

## Scene and timing script

### Shot O01, 00:00–00:12, establishing (12 seconds)

**Visual:** Wide office. Stephen cheerfully closes his suitcase. David scrutinises his laptop. Paul watches the printer as if expecting it to explain itself.

**On-screen location caption:** "Somewhere in an office. Unfortunately."

**PAUL** (blank): Is China far?

**STEPHEN:** Quite.

**PAUL:** Right.

A beat. Paul nods, having contributed all he intended.

### Shot O02, 00:12–00:45, Stephen's holiday (33 seconds)

**STEPHEN:** Right, I'm off. Two weeks. China. Romance. Culture. Absolutely no work.

**DAVID:** I've conducted a preliminary risk assessment.

**STEPHEN:** You've done what?

**DAVID:** International travel. Mysterious new boyfriend. Foreign territory. There are variables.

**STEPHEN:** The main variable is whether I get through a fortnight without either of you being weird.

**PAUL:** Do they do meal deals in China?

**STEPHEN:** I genuinely don't know, Paul.

**PAUL:** Could you check? No rush.

**Visual:** David turns his laptop to reveal an absurd document titled "OPERATION: PROBABLY FINE", with almost every field marked "HIGH RISK".

### Shot O03, 00:45–01:18, particular skills (33 seconds)

**DAVID:** If anything goes wrong, you call me. I possess a highly particular set of skills.

**STEPHEN:** You work in IT.

**DAVID:** Exactly.

**STEPHEN:** You reset passwords.

**DAVID:** At scale.

**PAUL:** You fixed the Wi-Fi once.

**DAVID:** Because I understand systems.

**PAUL:** I've been turning the fridge off and on. Is that IT?

**DAVID:** Why are you turning the fridge off?

**PAUL:** It was making a noise.

**Visual:** David stares. Stephen turns away to conceal a grin. Paul resumes his absolutely empty expression.

### Shot O04, 01:18–01:55, the printer incident (37 seconds)

**SFX:** Printer starts producing page after page. Paul collects the output and admires it.

**STEPHEN:** Why is the printer producing forty pictures of a bear?

**PAUL:** It's a famous bear.

**DAVID:** Paul, why have you put a bear next to the Chinese leader?

**PAUL:** I thought they'd like each other. They look quite similar.

**STEPHEN:** Please don't send those to me while I'm away.

**PAUL:** I've already made a group.

**Visual:** Fictional chat overlay appears: "**STEPHEN RESCUE GROUP (PRE-EMPTIVE)**". Three members; 1 bear sticker pending. Do not copy a protected commercial illustration. Use an original, generic cartoon honey-bear image if needed.

**DAVID:** Delete the group.

**PAUL:** Can't. I'm the admin.

**DAVID:** Then delete yourself.

**PAUL:** From the group?

**DAVID:** Yes, Paul. From the group.

**PAUL:** Right.

### Shot O05, 01:55–02:23, the goodbye song (28 seconds)

**DAVID:** We've also made you a goodbye song.

**STEPHEN:** Absolutely not.

**PAUL** (pressing play before anyone can stop him): Too late.

**AUDIO**, intentionally cheap two-line demo, badly timed and performed:

"Stephen's off to China, he'll probably be fine!
Unless he gets kidnapped, then that's a bad sign!"

**STEPHEN:** Is that meant to be comforting?

**DAVID:** That's the optimistic version.

**STEPHEN:** There is another version?

**PAUL:** It has a key change.

**DAVID:** And a post-mortem.

**STEPHEN:** Delete the song.

### Shot O06, 02:23–02:56, departure and punchline (33 seconds)

**Visual:** Stephen shoulders his bag. David rises theatrically. Paul is still clutching bear printouts.

**DAVID:** Send us one message every day so we know you're safe.

**STEPHEN:** I will be muting both of you before I reach the airport.

**PAUL:** Can you bring me back a souvenir?

**STEPHEN:** What do you want?

**PAUL:** A little fridge magnet, preferably with Wi-Fi.

**STEPHEN:** Goodbye, you absolute idiots.

**Visual:** Stephen exits. A phone notification appears: "Stephen Martin has muted the group" (clearly fictional interface).

**DAVID** (dead serious): That's our first warning sign.

**PAUL:** Should I buy a suitcase?

**DAVID:** For what?

**PAUL:** Rescue.

**DAVID:** We're not rescuing anyone, Paul.

**PAUL:** Yet.

### Shot O07, 02:56–03:08, smash-cut title (12 seconds)

**Visual:** David's laptop opens an enormous folder titled "**RESCUE PLAN – FINAL – FINAL 2**" on an obviously fictional operating-system desktop. A bright comic explosion wipe slams into the title:

# RESCUE STEPHEN MARTIN FROM CHINA

**Small subtitle:** "Two colleagues. Zero training. One completely avoidable situation."

**SFX:** Ridiculously overblown spy-thriller music hit.

**CUT TO:** \`STEVE-HOLIDAY-01\`, where the player takes control of Stephen.

The shot durations are initial editorial targets, not hard timers. Dialogue advances at the player's pace.

## Character direction

**Stephen:** Feels excited, witty and quick; he is aware his friends are idiots, not foolish in every situation. Use a hint of contemporary language but avoid constant recycled slang. He is amused until David's escalating paranoia threatens to ruin his trip.

**David:** Delivered like a self-important emergency briefing. Never intentionally plays the fool; his certainty is the joke. Standing posture, gestures, and his huge ego are as important as dialogue.

**Paul:** Underplay every line. Two seconds of blank stare after an absurd remark is usually funnier than a broad cartoon grimace. His illogical questions are completely sincere. White polo, glasses, clean-shaven, stocky.

## Editing and accessibility

- Every line has a named speaker and subtitle; no mandatory voice acting.
- Click/tap/space advances; Escape opens Skip/Resume for the cutscene, not a hard app exit.
- First viewing plays automatically. Repeat plays are optional from a scene/replay menu.
- On skip, mark \`openingSeen=true\`, move to \`STEVE-HOLIDAY-01\`; do not accidentally preselect a route.
- Keep the opening one scene with a handful of sprite/emotion changes. No need to model the entire office, animate walking or create bespoke art for every gag.
- Scene should be independently playable from the developer preview before connecting the rest of the adventure.
