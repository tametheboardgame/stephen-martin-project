# Creative bible

**Status:** Stephen, David and Paul visual baselines approved and committed under SP-WP1.1. Personality, dialogue and short narrative to be developed under SP-WP1.2.

## Privacy and reality-versus-fiction policy

Only **Stephen Martin** may appear as a real-world full name. David and Paul are first-name fictional character labels; no surnames, specific employer or office name, real travel dates, identifiable addresses, profiles or names of partners and relatives may enter the story, lyric drafts, comic dialogue, artwork metadata or publication materials.

The game uses a fictionalised holiday, invented romantic complications and fictional family/spy-thriller misunderstandings. No private details of real relationships, sexuality disclosures or relatives' awareness are established facts in the story bible. Any family visit, romantic relationship, disappearance, detention, cult or organ-market claim must be unmistakably comedic fiction, not reporting about a real person. Avoid replicating verbatim private messages.

The approved stylised character portraits remain the visual baseline, but public display of real-name artwork cannot be called anonymous. Perform a privacy check before publication.

## Tone
Adult absurdist British comedy, knowingly ridiculous international-espionage parody. The running gag is that David and Paul think they are action heroes but lack most of the necessary skills. Keep it funny, short and overtly fictional.

## Established core cast
- **Stephen Martin:** Fictionalised central character, depicted in his 20s. The character's enthusiasm for travel, romance and handsome adult men may lead others to misinterpret his disappearance. These are comedic story traits, **not published biographical assertions about any real person**. Visual baseline: `public/assets/generated/characters/stephen-martin-baseline.webp`, approved. Do not modify the approved character likeness without approval.
- **David:** In his 30s; visually approved under SP-WP1.1. Tall (6 ft 2 in / 188 cm), bald, blue-eyed, with a short dark ginger/auburn beard. His approved comic-sheet outfit is a dark charcoal hooded jacket over an olive-green T-shirt, jeans and casual trainers; a wristwatch is part of the illustrated reference. He is a capable IT specialist but ludicrously unqualified international rescuer: confident and determined until reality intervenes. Canonical asset: `public/assets/generated/characters/david-baseline.webp`. Visual gestures and expressions include crossed-arms determination, suspicious side-eye, smug confidence, chin-stroking analysis, frustration with hands on head, and finger-gun bravado. Clothing may vary by scene when explicitly agreed; retain face, build and recognisable mannerisms.
- **Paul:** Approved SP-WP1.1 comic character baseline. In his 40s, approximately 5 ft 10 in (178 cm), shorter and heavier-set than David; bald on top with very short hair stubble around the sides/back, **completely clean-shaven face**, dark rectangular glasses, a rounder face, and a white polo shirt (with contrasting dark trim in the approved reference), blue jeans and casual trainers. His defining comic reaction is a deadpan, blank or mildly perplexed look, with occasional confusion, bewilderment, stress and a nonplussed palms-up shrug. He should feel like an ordinary, slightly unprepared bloke and contrast David's showy overconfidence. Do not assign him an invented intelligence level or confirmed additional personality traits before SP-WP1.2. Canonical visual: `public/assets/generated/characters/paul-baseline.webp`.
- **The boyfriend:** Fictionalised ambiguous catalyst. Whether he's a spy, villain or simply normal is intentionally unresolved. Appearance: to be defined during story development if needed.

## Premise and potential resolutions
Stephen travels to China, then becomes unexpectedly difficult to locate. David and Paul embark on an unconvincing rescue. Possible endings: a mountain commune of adult admirers, escalating fictional state bureaucracy, and a cartoon black-market/body reconstruction. An optional reveal that Stephen engineered the situation remains a candidate, not mandatory.

## Continuity and framing
The game, song and optional comic use shared character designs and recognisable recurring jokes. Tone is satire; the story is not a factual allegation about real people or groups. Sexual innuendo and cartoon darkness may appear, but all suggestive characters must be adults and no explicit sexual depiction of real people should be created.
